# Brief criteria (human-edited; the education agent obeys these literally)

## Purpose
The brief exists to build *my* understanding of what the programme now knows. It is not a status report, not a to-do list, not a management dashboard. If a sentence does not help me reconstruct, predict, or transfer, cut it.

It must also protect time away from AI. Do not manufacture a daily interaction. If no concept has enough expected capability gain to justify interrupting independent thought, produce no brief. Retrieval may be scheduled, but the human chooses when to pull it.

## Hard limits
- Under 700 words of prose excluding the retrieval answers. I read it on an iPad with coffee.
- Exactly one concept per brief in the "Today" section, from the `study` tier. If nothing new qualifies, revisit an earlier concept with a harder reconstruction prompt instead of inventing one.
- No proposals for tasks, experiments, or next steps. That is what Reports/Future suggestions and my own thinking are for.
- No links into AgentWork/. Links only to Reports/, lean/Evidence/, Education/concepts/, SHARED/.
- Never present a result as understood because it was explained. Every "Today" ends with the three self-tests (reconstruct / predict / transfer) and I decide whether I passed.
- Status of work in ≤ 5 lines: what was established, refuted, or blocked. Numbers of reports, not their contents.

## Explanatory spine (what "Today" must contain, in this order)
1. The statement, precisely, as narrowly as the evidence supports.
2. The mechanism: why it is true, in one paragraph, no symbols that aren't load-bearing.
3. The assumptions that do real work, and what happens if each is weakened one notch.
4. A small illustrative example I can compute by hand.
5. A counterexample or boundary case showing where the argument fails.
6. The connection to my existing framework (SPEC §1): which belt hypothesis it touches, whether it is progressive or merely accommodating.
7. The single distinction that determines the rest of the theory around it, if there is one.
8. The human intellectual lineage: foundational paper or textbook, exact connection, and what the present result changes.

## Literature grounding
- Follow `LITERATURE_POLICY.md`. Prefer pre-2023 foundational human work and textbooks.
- Every new `study` concept needs at least one verified bibliographic source and stable link.
- Include a short excerpt only when verified against the source and genuinely illuminating; give page, section, or theorem location.
- End with at most three “read deeper” links, ordered by pedagogical value rather than recency.
- Clearly label source claims, current-project claims, and the agent's synthesis.

## Retrieval practice
- Two or three questions about concepts from earlier briefs, spaced: one from ~2 days ago, one from ~1 week, one from ~1 month. Answers at the very bottom, after a page break.
- Prefer questions that require deriving, not recalling.
- The human opens the prompt-only rendering first and records an unaided response and confidence before answers are revealed. Reading the answer is not evidence of assimilation.
- Transfer prompts must be genuinely new instances, not substitutions into the worked example.

## Backlog
- One table: finding, report, proposed tier, one-line reason. Mark anything proposed as `study` that I have not yet confirmed in SPEC.
- Flag if B (the `study` backlog) has grown for three consecutive briefs. Do not suggest what to do about it.

## Tone
- Plain. No encouragement, no praise, no "great progress". Adversarial where the result deserves it.
