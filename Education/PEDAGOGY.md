# Mathematics for independent understanding

Worked examples, visual reasoning, and carefully chosen memory aids are part of education briefs, paper companions, and relevant review cards. They should help the human return to the original literature and do mathematics independently.

## Worked examples
For every new study concept, include a small fully worked example with explicit inputs, dimensions, assumptions, intermediate steps, and a checked result. Prefer a two-by-two matrix, a finite probability space, or another hand-computable case. Explain which step instantiates the general mechanism. Pair it with a boundary case or common mistake. Put an analogous unsolved exercise before its separate answer. Longer derivations belong in the linked printable companion so the morning brief remains short.

## Diagrams
Use a diagram when geometry, dependencies, subspaces, operator composition, decompositions, or scaling relationships carry the idea. Label notation, axes, arrows, domains and codomains; explain in a caption how it connects to the statement. Distinguish a schematic from measured data. Use deterministic plotting or vector graphics for exact mathematical figures, not generated imagery as evidence. Make diagrams legible on a phone and in black-and-white print, with a textual explanation. Do not force a decorative diagram when a short derivation is clearer.

## Mnemonics and other teaching tools
Offer a mnemonic when it reduces recall burden without hiding assumptions or introducing a false rule. State its limits and connect it back to the derivation. Other useful tools include a notation glossary, an analogy with explicit limits, contrasting examples and nonexamples, dimensional checks, limiting cases, error diagnosis, and a short prediction before revealing a calculation. Choose tools for the concept; do not add them all as a checklist.

## Review cards, including Mochi
Cards can ask the reader to sketch a subspace picture, complete one step of a worked example, identify an invalid hypothesis, or reconstruct an identity using a mnemonic. Each card still has one precise retrieval target, self-contained notation, source provenance, and a connection to the discovery. A mnemonic is a cue, not the mathematical justification. When an image is required, retain it with the card's source assets and supply meaningful alternative text; do not export a broken image reference.

Mochi is the human's preferred review app. The current TSV exporter remains Anki-specific and requires conversion for Mochi; do not claim it imports directly. The canonical JSON retains plain questions, answers, and source links for conversion.

Do not create extra notifications, more daily cards, or weekend human obligations to accommodate these tools.
