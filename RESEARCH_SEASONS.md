# Research seasons

The same research programme needs different agent behavior at different times. `SPEC.md` names the active season; scheduled runners read it before acting. A season changes only by explicit human decision.

## 1. Study and theory formation

**Human priority:** reading, thinking, developing definitions, intuition, conjectures, and proofs.

**Agent role:** protect uninterrupted thought; locate foundational literature; clarify notation and dependencies; construct small examples and counterexamples only when requested. Do not flood the human with generated theorems or pre-empt their mathematical work.

**Success:** the human can state the mechanism and generate the next conjecture independently.

## 2. Empirical discovery and validation

**Human priority:** decide what the theory is really claiming and which observations would matter.

**Agent role:** translate claims into severe, minimal experiments; identify discriminating controls; launch only explicitly authorized goals; reduce broad experimental programmes toward the smallest test that separates the live hypotheses.

**Success:** concrete operational quantities, a small matched test, and a result that could genuinely refute the theory.

## 3. Mathematical consolidation

**Human priority:** return to the mathematics, reform concepts in light of evidence, and develop the human-owned theorem.

**Agent role:** organize counterexamples, assumption dependencies, proof obligations, and foundational connections. Agents support rather than replace theorem creation.

**Success:** a small statement with load-bearing assumptions and a human-reconstructable proof strategy.

## 4. Rigorous confirmation and paper reduction

**Human priority:** judgment about the paper's central contribution and exposition.

**Agent role:** independent replication, robustness checks, formalization, provenance, and aggressive deletion of nonessential claims and experiments.

**Success:** one or two central claims, the smallest convincing experiments, minimal mathematics, and an auditable evidence trail.

## Season-sensitive scheduling

The morning and afternoon runners remain fixed; their behavior changes with the active season. They never create a new goal. The human explicitly assigns goals each morning and marks which work belongs to agents versus the human. Morning planning proposes but does not launch experiments. The afternoon may resume, diagnose, or subdivide already-authorized goals without changing their scientific target.
